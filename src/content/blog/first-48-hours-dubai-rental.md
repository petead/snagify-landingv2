Congratulations, the keys are in your hand. The next 48 hours are a strange window: everything feels urgent, movers, internet, that first grocery run, and yet almost everything on your list can slip a day without consequence.

Almost everything. One task is genuinely time-critical, most people skip it, and it decides what happens to your deposit twelve months from now. Here is the full playbook, in the order that actually matters.

## Hour zero: the empty apartment is evidence

Before a single box crosses the threshold, walk the property with your phone and document everything: every wall, floor, ceiling, door, window, appliance, bathroom fixture, and the AC units. Open every cupboard. Run every tap. Photograph the DEWA meter reading while you are at it.

Why now and not this weekend? Because an empty apartment is the cleanest condition baseline you will ever have. Once furniture arrives, walls disappear behind sofas, floors go under rugs, and every scratch discovered later becomes ambiguous: was it there before, or did the movers do it? The empty-apartment window lasts a day or two and never comes back.

And in Dubai, this job is entirely yours. As we covered in detail, no law requires a check-in report here, no landlord is obligated to provide one, and most will not. The Rental Disputes Center decides deposit cases on evidence of original condition, and the system leaves creating that evidence to you. Make the record structured and time-stamped, room by room rather than a loose camera roll, get it signed by the landlord or agent if at all possible, and if they will not sign, email them the full report today so a dated, unobjected-to record exists. Twenty minutes, and the single biggest risk of your tenancy is neutralized.

While you walk, keep a second list: everything that is already wrong. The scratch on the bedroom door, the stiff balcony lock, the stained grout. Report it in writing to the landlord or agent within these first days, with photos. The sooner a defect is on the record, the harder it is to bill you for it at check-out, and silence is exactly what converts the previous tenant's damage into your problem.

## Day one: power, water, cooling

**DEWA.** Nothing else works without it. Apply online or through the DEWA app with your Ejari number, Emirates ID and passport copy; the security deposit is AED 2,000 for an apartment, AED 4,000 for a villa, refundable when you close the account at the end of the tenancy. Activation usually lands within 24 hours, often same day. Photograph the opening meter reading and keep it with your condition report: it is the other baseline of your tenancy.

**District cooling, if your building has it.** In many towers, AC is not DEWA but a separate provider like Empower or Emicool, with its own registration, its own deposit, and its own final bill at move-out. Ask the building management which applies. Skipping this registration is the classic reason a new tenant spends their first Dubai summer weekend without cooling.

**Gas, if applicable.** Some buildings run centralized gas with providers requiring their own connection. Same reflex: ask management.

## Day one, in parallel: the building's own rules

**The move-in permit.** Most towers and gated communities will not let a moving truck through the gate without a move-in permit or NOC from building management, often requested 24 to 48 hours in advance, sometimes with a refundable deposit for the service elevator. If your movers are booked for tomorrow and you have no permit, fix that within the hour. This is the most common day-one ambush in Dubai buildings.

**Access inventory.** Count and note everything you were handed: keys, access cards, parking remotes, mailbox keys. They will be counted again at check-out, and the missing-remote charge is a small classic of deposit deductions. Put the counts in your condition report.

## Day two: paperwork and comfort

**Ejari, if not already done.** Your tenancy contract must be registered in Ejari, usually handled by the agent, sometimes left to you. Without it there is no DEWA activation, and no standing at the RDC if a dispute ever arises. If it is not done by day two, chase it in writing.

**Internet.** du or e& (Etisalat), with installation slots typically a few days out, so book early. Check whether your building is covered by one provider exclusively, a common Dubai quirk.

**Test everything under load.** First shower at full heat, washing machine on a real cycle, oven, every AC zone on a hot afternoon. Anything that fails goes on the written defects list immediately, while attribution is still obvious. Most Dubai contracts make minor maintenance yours below a threshold (AED 500 is a common figure) and the landlord's above it, so knowing what is broken on day two, in writing, matters financially.

## The 48-hour scorecard

By the end of day two you should have: a structured, dated, ideally signed condition report of the empty apartment, the meter reading photographed, every pre-existing defect reported in writing, DEWA and cooling active, the move-in permit sorted, keys and access counted, and Ejari confirmed.

Everything else, the furniture layout, the curtains, the sixth trip to the supermarket, is life, and life can take its time. The list above is the tenancy. Two days of small diligence now, and in twelve months your check-out is a formality instead of a negotiation, with your deposit coming back on evidence, not on goodwill.
